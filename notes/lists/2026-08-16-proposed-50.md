# Fifty open problems for short AI + Lean + CAS campaigns

I optimized for crisp statements with a plausible *dent*: a new finite case, a certified construction or obstruction, a better exponent or constant, or a reusable formal lemma. The status cutoff is 2026-08-16. I favored living surveys and primary papers, and screened out several newly stale targets (including non-sofic-group existence, the old real sum-product conjecture, Erdős–Hajnal for graphs on at most five vertices, lonely runner through 13 runners, and the recently announced order-668 Hadamard construction). “Open” below means that the cited current source still treats the stated target as open and I found no later accepted resolution; where that check is less decisive, I say so explicitly. A dent need not settle the headline conjecture.

### P01. Quantitative arithmetic removal in the integers
- Slug: quantitative-arithmetic-removal
- Area: Additive combinatorics
- Why open: If (A\subseteq[N]) has at most $\varepsilon N^2$ solutions to (x+y=z), the arithmetic removal lemma gives deletion of (o_\varepsilon(N)) elements to make (A) sum-free, but no remotely satisfactory dependence of the deletion fraction on $\varepsilon$ is known.
- Attack shape: bound
- Why an AI stack might dent it: Finite cyclic models, energy increment recurrences, and exact optimization of small cases can be explored computationally, while Lean can certify a quantitatively improved chain of inequalities.
- Sources: [Ben Green, *100 Open Problems*, Problem 8 (December 2025 update)](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf); [Green, *A Szemerédi-type regularity lemma in abelian groups*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-005-0509-8)

### P02. A logarithmic saving for five-term progression-free sets
- Slug: five-term-progression-log-saving
- Area: Additive combinatorics
- Why open: Writing (r_5(N)) for the largest subset of ([N]) with no nontrivial five-term arithmetic progression, it is still unknown whether (r_5(N)\ll N(\log N)^{-c}) for some absolute (c>0).
- Attack shape: bound
- Why an AI stack might dent it: A campaign can isolate one quantitative loss in the (U^4)-inverse/density-increment pipeline, test finite-field analogues, and formalize the combinatorial bookkeeping that often hides improvable constants.
- Sources: [Ben Green, *100 Open Problems*, Problem 9(ii)](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf); [Gowers, *A new proof of Szemerédi's theorem*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002220100205)

### P03. Four-term progressions in Fourier-uniform sets
- Slug: four-ap-fourier-uniform
- Area: Additive combinatorics
- Why open: For a density-$\alpha$ set (A\subseteq\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z) with all nontrivial Fourier coefficients (o(N)), it is unknown whether (A) must contain at least (\gg \alpha^{100}N^2) four-term arithmetic progressions.
- Attack shape: counterexample
- Why an AI stack might dent it: Structured two-step nilsequence families can be parameter-searched for counterexamples, with exact rational or algebraic certificates replacing floating-point evidence.
- Sources: [Ben Green, *100 Open Problems*, Problem 13](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf); [Gowers, *A new proof of Szemerédi's theorem*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002220100205)

### P04. The Möbius-ladder Cayley–Sidorenko inequality
- Slug: mobius-ladder-cayley-sidorenko
- Area: Extremal graph theory / additive combinatorics
- Why open: The Cayley-graph instance of Sidorenko's conjecture for the first unresolved graph (K_{5,5}\setminus C_{10}) asks for the corresponding ten-variable configuration count to be at least its random-density value; this special inequality remains open.
- Attack shape: proof
- Why an AI stack might dent it: The target is a finite multilinear inequality amenable to sum-of-squares searches, flag-algebra experiments, and Lean verification of a discovered Cauchy–Schwarz certificate.
- Sources: [Ben Green, *100 Open Problems*, Problem 12](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf); [Conlon, Fox, and Sudakov, *An approximate version of Sidorenko's conjecture*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00493-010-2396-7)
