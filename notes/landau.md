# Landau's four problems (1912)

Edmund Landau, ICM Cambridge 1912. Four prime questions he called
"unattackable at the present state of mathematics."

Status cutoff 2026-08-17: all four still open.
Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau%27s_problems).

| # | Problem | Status | What is known |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Goldbach: every even \(n>2\) is \(p+q\) | Open | Weak (ternary) Goldbach: Helfgott 2013. Chen: large even \(n=p+P_2\). Exceptional set density zero (Montgomery–Vaughan); Pintz \(E(x)<x^{0.72}\). |
| 2 | Twin primes: infinitely many \(p\) with \(p+2\) prime | Open | Zhang 2013 bounded gaps; Polymath to 246. Chen primes: infinitely many \(p\) with \(p+2\) prime or semiprime. |
| 3 | Legendre: a prime in \((n^2,(n+1)^2)\) for every \(n\) | Open | Equivalent to gaps after \(p\) smaller than \(2\sqrt{p}\). Checked through \(2^{64}\) via maximal-gap tables. Ingham: a prime between cubes for large \(n\). |
| 4 | Infinitely many primes \(n^2+1\) | Open | OEIS [A002496](https://oeis.org/A002496). Iwaniec 1978: infinitely many \(n^2+1\) with at most two prime factors. Friedlander–Iwaniec: infinitely many \(x^2+y^4\). Grimmelt–Merikoski 2025: largest prime factor of \(n^2+1\) is \(\ge n^{1.312}\) i.o. Brun: \(O(\sqrt{x}/\log x)\) such primes up to \(x\). |

Unrefereed 2026 preprints claiming a proof of (4) are not treated as resolutions.

Hilbert 8 asked for Goldbach and twins as special cases. These four are the
smallest classical list that is still entirely open.
